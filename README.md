## ANALYSIS OF FOOD WASTE IN THE UNITED STATES

![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9de9a1be-8a8b-4e98-a9c8-74b8ff197dea)


This project analyzes food waste in the United States, focusing on its sources, reasons for waste, environmental impacts, and potential solutions. By understanding the magnitude and origins of food waste, this analysis aims to inform policy and programs that tackle food insecurity and reduce waste.

## 📌 Overview

Every year, the United States wastes over **963 million** tons of food, despite widespread food insecurity, environmental strain, and growing sustainability concerns. This project explores the **magnitude, causes, sources, destinations, and environmental impact** of food surplus and waste in the U.S., using national-level data provided by [ReFED.org](https://refed.org/).

## 🎯 Project Goals

- Quantify the scale and value of food surplus and waste in the U.S.
- Identify the key sectors and food types contributing to food waste
- Analyze the causes of waste across the supply chain
- Explore waste disposal destinations (e.g., landfill, composting)
- Estimate environmental impacts (CO₂ emissions and water usage)
- Provide actionable insights for policy makers, NGOs, and sustainability advocates


## 🧩 Data Sources

Three CSV files were sourced from [ReFED.org](https://refed.org/):

- `REFED_US_FOOD_SURPLUS_SUMMARY.csv` — National surplus and waste by year and sector  
- `REFED_US_STATE_FOOD_SURPLUS_SUMMARY.csv` — State-level food surplus and waste  
- `REFED_US_FOOD_SURPLUS_CAUSE_SUMMARY.csv` — Causes of surplus and food not fit for consumption

## 📊 Dashboard Highlights

### 1. Waste Quantity

- Estimated **$5 trillion** in surplus food over the period
- **87% of surplus** food was never consumed
- U.S. generated **963M tons** of food waste (2010–2022)

![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d05b3159-2ea2-4dcc-bf3b-83d34807133a)


### 2.  Waste Sources 

- **Residential households** contributed 54% of waste
- Major waste contributors: **farm produce**, **prepared foods**, and **dairy**
- Consumer-facing sectors (homes + foodservice) account for **80%** of waste

![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b7b09599-a4d1-41bc-9e92-f71a9b9e4aa2)


### 3.  Waste Causes

- Key causes:
  - **Trimmings & Byproducts** (e.g., food processing waste)
  - **Spoilage**
  - **Food safety concerns** (e.g., date label confusion)
- Waste occurs from farm to retail, with specific patterns by sector

![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a40ed2d2-e22f-42bd-8276-bb1e7e607b90)


### 4.  Waste Destinations

- **Landfill** is the #1 destination for wasted food.
- **80%** of food waste derived from consumer-facing businesses and consumers are channeled to landfills.
- Low rates of composting, donation, or anaerobic digestion.

![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e343db34-0dca-46b6-bbc3-449f1e066105)

### 5.  Environmental Impacts

- **263 billion gallons** of water used on wasted food
- **4.67 billion metric tons** of CO₂ emissions  
- Top environmental offenders: **prepared foods**, **dairy**, **meat & seafood**

![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9c5f8b91-5780-4030-890d-baf845170f11)

## 📈 Impact

This project equips:
- **Policy makers** with visibility into waste-heavy regions and sectors  
- **NGOs and advocates** with data to support food recovery programs  
- **Sustainability teams** with insight into where intervention can reduce waste and carbon impact

It directly supports **UN Sustainable Development Goal 12.3**:  
> *“By 2030, halve per capita global food waste at the retail and consumer levels and reduce food losses along production and supply chains.”*

### CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

This analysis highlights the urgent need for integrated strategies to reduce food waste in the U.S. Effective measures could significantly mitigate environmental impacts and enhance food security. Such measures include:

 - Prevention and Reduction: Implementing sustainable farming practices and improving the food supply chains.
 - Recovery and Redistribution: Establishing food collection networks and incentivizing food donations.
 - Recycling and Composting: Promoting community composting programs and biogas production facilities.
